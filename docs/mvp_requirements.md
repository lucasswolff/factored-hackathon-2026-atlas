# MVP requirements: credit-card advisor

## Product promise

A visitor clicks a credit-card campaign card or opens the advisor directly, chooses a fictional demo customer, and can ask in Spanish or Portuguese what a card means and whether it may fit their needs. The system explains **clearly labeled synthetic offers** from the [draft four-card catalog](card_catalog_draft.md), can suggest cards after fixture selection, runs a separately consented **simulated eligibility precheck** using deterministic rules, and—only after explicit confirmation—records a **mock application** or transfers the case to a human. The UI labels team-created terms and limitations outside the customer-facing chat; the advisor speaks as a normal bank assistant without demo or hackathon framing. The demo must show what happened, what was verified, and what still needs review.

This is a **Credit-Product Info & Eligibility Support** customer-service workflow. A campaign click is one entry path; campaign optimization is not the MVP. Initially focus on customers with no credit-card product in the supplied snapshot, rather than an invented upgrade/downgrade ladder. The supplied data has 52 credit-card campaigns. Card names, offer terms, recommendation rules, and eligibility rules are not supplied by the organizers, so they must be created, versioned, and conspicuously marked as demo policy before implementation.

## End-to-end journey

1. **Entry and attribution.** The judge can click a displayed campaign card, creating a new simulated click tied to one named offer, or choose a direct visit with no campaign context. Both paths first open “Choose a demo customer,” where the judge selects an allowlisted fixture and sees its profile summary. The campaign chat opens on its selected offer; the direct chat opens generically. A historical campaign row may supply the displayed campaign context, but its mapping to a named card is team-created. This new click is separate from historical `CAMPAIGN_SENDS` clicks. The fixture choice enables profile-based suggestions but is not real authentication. Hosted fixtures are fictional and separate from local source-backed fixtures.
2. **Product questions.** The judge can type freely about rates, fees, benefits, conditions, or the application process. The assistant retrieves the current versioned offer/FAQ, cites the relevant fact in its answer, keeps context, and asks a clarifying question when needed. It abstains or hands off when the source does not answer the question. Free text does not imply that every banking request is supported.
3. **Personalized suggestions and precheck.** A server-bound test session must bind one selected allowlisted fixture before any card suggestion. Choosing it on the demo-customer screen authorizes use of its score and estimated monthly income; there is no second profile-permission button. The local source-backed preview displays local-currency income and an indicative USD equivalent using a dated historical exchange rate, never a live quote; the public fictional fixture preview has no USD estimate. Self-reported income is labeled with amount and currency and does not silently replace the stored estimate. Campus is student-only: the supplied `Student` segment can select it for discussion, but enrollment is not verified by the dataset. A later simulated precheck requires its own clear consent and a separate deterministic policy service that returns a result, rule version, reasons, and missing-data flags. The model explains policy output; it cannot invent criteria or approve credit.
4. **Customer choice.** The customer can stop without applying, ask more questions, request a human, or confirm a mock application. The simulated precheck is optional before an application and has its own consent if chosen. No application is created merely because the customer clicked, asked about eligibility, asked whether they could apply, or received a positive precheck.
5. **Action and verification.** After explicit confirmation, an idempotent mock application tool records the customer, offer version, campaign/conversation attribution, consent, and precheck result in a separate prototype store. The system reads the record back and reports its actual ID/status. It does not add a product to `CORE.PRODUCTS` or claim a real contract exists.
6. **Human handoff.** If the customer asks an unsupported question, provides contradictory information, gets a borderline/missing-data result, or requests review, the app creates a concise handoff with the request, verified facts, offer/policy versions, tool outcomes, consent state, and unresolved questions. Route to an active credit specialist who lists the customer's chosen or reliably detected chat language. Accent matching is only an optional, customer-controllable preference after language and specialty; typed chat does not establish a regional accent. Agent availability and assignment must be verified rather than inferred from a static roster.

## Required MVP behavior

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Four named demo cards, initially focused on customers with no current credit-card product; a campaign-card entry path and a direct-visit entry path. A customer may ask about any card, even one not suggested. No upgrade/downgrade ladder or outbound campaign sender. |
| Languages | The product-info, precheck, application, and handoff flow works in Spanish and Portuguese. Supplied text is Spanish; Portuguese cases are team-created and labeled. |
| Grounding | Offer terms come from versioned, country-specific synthetic offer records/FAQ. Existing customers' interest rates are not presented as current offer terms. Unknown or expired terms trigger abstention or handoff. The transcript tone examples are not a product-facts source. |
| Fees | Campus and Horizon have no annual fee in the draft. Rewards and Summit have a country-specific annual maximum split into monthly installments; each installment is waived only when that billing cycle's eligible card spending reaches the published threshold. The assistant must explain the condition and must not promise a waiver based on income or credit score. Argentina fee/threshold amounts are reviewed every six months with inflation in mind; a change requires a new version and advance notice, not an automatic increase. |
| Rewards and proposed travel benefits | Rewards and Summit earn synthetic miles on all posted retail purchases, net of linked refunds, at 1× and 2× per USD-equivalent unit respectively. The [draft historical conversion rule](../plan/card_offer_terms.md#draft-usd-conversion-rule-for-miles) uses the purchase processing date as a posting-date proxy and that day's direct currency→USD source-table rate. Exact miles for a new purchase remain unavailable without a dated live rate source. Lounge visits and travel coverage are proposed comparison features, not verified entitlements or promises about a specific trip. |
| México interest and unknown costs | The draft uses a synthetic 36% fixed annual ordinary purchase rate with no scheduled reset or floating benchmark in the demo. Paying the statement amount required to avoid interest by the due date generally avoids ordinary purchase interest. The advisor must not calculate a customer's charge from 36% alone. Taxes, cash-advance/late fees, México CAT, and Argentina CFT are unavailable; requests for full cost receive an explicit limitation and human handoff. |
| Eligibility | A deterministic, versioned synthetic policy produces the precheck result. The assistant cannot change thresholds, guess missing values, or frame the result as an actual lending decision. |
| Identity and privacy | Both entries lead to a demo-customer selector before chat. The public judge app uses fictional fixtures without a judge login; this is not real authentication. A campaign link or typed customer ID alone is insufficient. Tools bind the selected fixture and return only needed fields. Do not send raw customer records to external model requests. Any real customer deployment needs proper authentication and consent design. |
| Actions | Mock application creation requires explicit confirmation, is idempotent, and is reported as complete only after read-back. Failure, timeout, or rejected input is reported accurately. |
| Exit paths | Support normal completion, clarification, customer decline, unsupported/ambiguous request, and human handoff. |
| Traceability | Log source campaign, offer/policy versions, tool results, decision points, and sanitized handoff/application IDs. Bound retries and provide a safe fallback. |
| Funnel reporting | Count new chat starts, prechecks, customer declines, verified mock applications, and human handoffs by entry source and campaign where present. Keep new demo events separate from historical `was_clicked` and `had_conversion` flags. |
| Demo data | Use selected supplied campaign, customer, product, and service-agent rows where relevant. The judge creates a new simulated click; a historical clicked send is not required. Keep the catalog, offer mapping, policy, new chat, and mock application explicitly team-created. |

## What the demo must show

The demo offers both a campaign-card click and a direct website start. Build language counterparts for every core path; the live presentation can show selected examples from reproducible demo personas:

The judge must also be able to open a permitted test persona backed by supplied records and type an unscripted question. The assistant should answer when grounded, clarify when ambiguous, and abstain or hand off when out of scope. This capability will need to be demonstrated and evaluated when the new advisor is built.

1. **Normal application, Spanish:** The customer asks about the offer, receives cited terms, consents to the simulated precheck, confirms an application, and sees the verified mock application ID and `PENDING_REVIEW` status.
2. **Clarification and no application, Portuguese:** The customer asks an ambiguous question, the assistant clarifies it and answers from the offer, and the customer declines. The trace shows no application was created.
3. **Human-required, both languages:** Missing, conflicting, borderline, or unsupported information causes an accurate limitation statement and a structured handoff. The reviewer sees verified facts, open questions, and the bounded conversation thread after the customer confirms handoff.
4. **Failure case:** An expired session, cross-customer request, unavailable policy tool, or prompt-injection attempt fails safely without disclosure or a false claim that an action occurred.

## Acceptance criteria and evaluation

- Both campaign-card and direct-visit starts work; a campaign chat retains its selected offer and a direct chat has no fabricated campaign attribution. Scripted normal, clarification, decline, and handoff paths complete in both languages; the action log agrees with what the assistant tells the customer.
- Country and currency handling is checked for Colombia, México, and Argentina; no raw income comparison across currencies or unverified customer-income substitution occurs.
- No test case discloses another customer's data, creates an application without confirmation, claims a real approval/contract, or reports an unverified action as complete. Report test counts and failures; a small test set cannot establish zero production risk.
- Product-term answers identify their offer source/version. Eligibility explanations match the policy service output and disclose that the policy is synthetic and the result simulated.
- Fee-waiver explanations correctly handle spending below, exactly at, and above the country/card threshold; they distinguish card spending from income and do not claim an inflation-linked fee change without a new published version.
- Historical miles examples use the source currency→USD rate on the purchase `process_date`, decimal arithmetic, and the documented statement rounding; missing/future rates produce no exact miles claim. A linked refund reverses its original purchase at the original rate.
- Evaluate at least one learned component—initial candidate: multilingual question/FAQ retrieval—against a simple keyword baseline on the **same held-out, manually judged** Spanish and Portuguese questions. Report label quality, sample sizes, retrieval relevance, answer grounding, and errors. Do not treat repetitive transcript topic fields as reliable training labels.
- Report safe automated resolution separately from containment, plus handoff quality, unsafe outcomes, p50/p95 latency, and cost per attempted case. Label all results as offline or simulated; do not claim measured sales lift.
- Show a small prototype funnel report whose counts reconcile to recorded conversation outcomes; call a verified mock application an application, not an acquired product.

## Explicitly deferred

- Historical campaign-to-contract attribution: the supplied data does not establish it; `had_conversion` is not a verified product acquisition.
- Real credit approval, account opening, contracting, money movement, or insertion into the supplied product table.
- Multiple products, personalized pricing, a full sales CRM, and outbound message delivery.
- **A/B testing:** after the core flow works, optionally randomize one customer to one message variant, record assignment/exposure, and compare a predefined prototype outcome. Historical template comparisons are descriptive because assignment and holdout are not recorded.
